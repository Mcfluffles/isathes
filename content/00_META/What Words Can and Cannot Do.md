This document defines the **operational limits** of language in Old Empyrean.

### What Words Can Do

Words can:

- **Acknowledge reality**  
    State conditions that already exist, including internal states.
- **Clarify intent**  
    Make will explicit, reducing ambiguity for self and others.
- **Stabilize understanding**  
    When speaker and reality are aligned, words reinforce coherence.
- **Bind the speaker cognitively**  
    Once spoken truthfully, certain statements constrain future action without external enforcement.
- **Be used as evidence**  
    Not magically, but socially and psychologically.

### What Words Cannot Do

Words cannot:

- **Create reality**  
    No utterance alters the world by itself.
- **Compel others**  
    Commands have no inherent authority.
- **Impose obligation**  
    Obligation must preexist the statement.
- **Replace intent**  
    Speaking without alignment produces no effect beyond internal consequence.
- **Override contradiction**  
    No phrasing can bypass falsehood.
### Silence as a Valid State

Silence is not absence of language.  
It is a **refusal to anchor**.

In Old Empyrean philosophy, choosing not to speak may be the most accurate statement available.
