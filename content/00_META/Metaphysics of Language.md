In Old Empyrean metaphysics, language is not symbolic.  
It is **referential**.

Words do not represent ideas.  
They point to _states of being, intent, or perception_ that already exist.

### Words as Anchors

Each utterance functions as an anchor between:

- the speaker’s internal state
- an external or conceptual truth

If alignment exists, the word stabilizes understanding.  
If alignment does not exist, the word destabilizes the speaker.

This is why falsehood is dangerous—not morally, but structurally.

### Truth Is Not Moral

Old Empyrean does not encode moral truth.

A statement may be:

- accurate
- inaccurate
- incomplete
- misaligned

But never “sinful” or “virtuous.”

Consequences arise from **misalignment**, not judgment.

### Will, Not Force

The metaphysical system recognizes **will** as a property of conscious beings, not as an external force.

Language can:

- acknowledge will
- delimit will
- clarify will

But it cannot create will.

Verbs of intent (_unel_, etc.) are statements of **internal state**, not acts of causation.

### Obligation Is External

Obligation verbs (_vaeth_, related forms) never originate in the speaker.

They reference:

- prior commitments
- imposed constraints
- metaphysical bindings already in effect

Attempting to assert false obligation results in internal contradiction, often perceived as psychological or cognitive strain.

### Perception Is Not Knowledge

Perceptive verbs encode _exposure_, not certainty.

To perceive is not to understand.  
To understand is not to know.  
To know is not to be correct.

The language carefully separates these layers to prevent the false authority of speech.
