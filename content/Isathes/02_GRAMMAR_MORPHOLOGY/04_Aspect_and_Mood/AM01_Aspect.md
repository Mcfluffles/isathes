# Aspect in Isathes

## 1. General Orientation

> **Aspect in Isathes describes how states and events hold, recur, or resolve over time.**  
> The language privileges **endurance, stability, and completion** over motion or process.

Aspect is distinct from tense and mood:

- **Tense** locates an event in time.
- **Aspect** describes its temporal _shape_.
- **Mood** encodes agency and causation.

Aspect applies primarily to:

- states
- bindings
- nominalized events

Aspect does **not** stack.

---

## 2. Endurance-Based Aspect (Core System)

Isathes is a **state-oriented** language. Its core aspectual distinctions are built around endurance.

### Aspect particles

|Particle|Meaning|Use|
|---|---|---|
|**ren**|ongoing / currently holding|active, unstable, or maintained states|
|**sel**|habitual / characteristic|what is generally or normally true|
|**sha**|inceptive|beginning to hold|

### Examples

`Vaethen ren kisal`

> The binding currently holds.

`[Peace] sel kisal`

> Peace is generally the case.

`[Truce] sha kisal`

> The truce is beginning to hold.

---

## 3. Progressive Meaning (Derived, Not Grammatical)

**Isathes has no dedicated progressive aspect.**

Progressive meaning (“is happening right now”) is **derived**, not marked.

---

### Progressive derivation rule

> **Progressive meaning is expressed by treating an event as a temporary state and asserting its current existence using endurance aspect.**

### Core patterns

`[Event nominal] ren kisal 
`[Entity] ve [event nominal] ren kisal`

---

### Examples

**“The city is being attacked”**

`[City] ve [ATTACK-NOM] ren kisal`

Literal sense:

> An attack-state currently exists upon the city.

**“The binding is being formed (not yet complete)”**

`Vaethen sha kisal`

Progressive meaning is achieved without:

- special verb forms
- motion-oriented grammar
- process marking

---

## 4. Iterative Aspect (Event Repetition)

### Definition

> **Iterative aspect describes the repeated occurrence of an event across a defined temporal span.**

It is:

- event-based
- distributive
- non-habitual
- non-continuous

---

### Iterative particle: **`sen`**

**`sen`** marks distributive repetition (“each / every / per”).

It does **not** imply:

- continuity
- inevitability
- inherent nature

---

### Canon structure

`[Subject] sen [time-span] [event verb]`

Where:

- **time-span** is a temporal noun or pronoun (day, year, cycle, etc.)
- the verb remains unmodified

---

### Examples

**“The city is attacked every year”**

`[City] sen [YEAR] [ATTACK-V]

**“They meet each night”**

`ad sen [NIGHT] [MEET-V]`

**“The rite is performed every cycle”**

`[Rite] sen [CYCLE] [PERFORM-V]`

---

### Contrast with habitual (`sel`)

| Construction                 | Meaning                        |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `[City] sel [peaceful]`      | The city is generally peaceful |
| `[City] sen [year] [attack]` | The city is attacked each year |

Habitual describes **states**.  
Iterative describes **events**.

They do not overlap.

---

## 5. Telicity (Completion vs Non-Completion)

### Core principle

> **Telicity in Isathes is resolved through the existence or non-existence of a result-state, not through verb morphology.**

An event is telic if it can produce a result-state.

---

### Canon telicity rule

- **Process nominal** → attempt or ongoing action
- **Result nominal + `kisal`** → successful completion
- **Failure nominal + `[POESSESION-P]` + `kisal`** → unsuccessful completion

Failure is treated as a **state belonging to the attempted event**, expressed via possession.

---

### Examples (Class I)

**Ongoing attempt**

`Vaethir ren kisal`

**Successful completion**

`Vaethen kisal`

**Failed completion**

`Vaethir POS brethsalir kisal`

> The failure belonging to the act of binding exists.

This distinguishes:

- doing
- finishing
- failing

---

## 6. Aspectual Scope Constraints

- Aspect applies to:
    - states
    - bindings
    - nominalized events
- Aspect does **not** apply directly to invocative mood.
- Aspect particles do **not** stack.

---

## 7. Cultural Implication

Aspect in Isathes reflects a worldview concerned with:

- whether things **hold**
- whether they **fail**
- whether they **recur**
- whether they **complete**

Not with how they _move_.
