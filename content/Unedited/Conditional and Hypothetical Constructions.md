# Conditional & Hypothetical Constructions (Isathes) — **Canon**

## 1. Core Conditional Structure

All conditionals use **result-first order**:

`[Result Clause] [CONDITIONAL PARTICLE] [Condition Clause]`

Gloss:

> X is the resultant condition, **if / given / under** Y holds.

Conditionals **assert dependency**, not causation.

---

## 2. Universal Constraints

- **Invocative mood** is **forbidden** in both clauses.
    
- **Imperative mood** is **forbidden** in the **condition clause**.
    
- Default tense is **non-past**.
    
- **Past tense in the condition** expresses **counterfactual / unreal** meaning.
    
- Conditional particles **do not stack**.
    

---

## 3. Conditional Particles (Distinct Domains)

Isathes distinguishes three conditional particles with **non-overlapping semantic roles**.

### 3.1 **sare** — Factual / Contingent Conditional

_(“given that”)_

**Meaning:** The result applies **if the condition in fact holds**.  
**Domain:** logical, factual, situational (default conditional).  
**Use:** practical reasoning; neutral conditionals; many requests.

**Form**

`X sare Y`

**Example**

`Talir kisal sare thy help`

> Ability exists, if you help.  
> → _If you help, I can._

---

### 3.2 **kel** — Evaluative / Social Conditional

_(“on the condition that”)_

**Meaning:** The result is **evaluated or offered** under the condition, without authority or pressure.  
**Domain:** social negotiation, politeness, proposals.  
**Use:** requests, offers, soft persuasion.

**Form**

`X kel Y`

**Example**

`I glad kisal kel thy come`

> I would be glad, on the condition that you come.  
> → _Could you come?_

---

### 3.3 **nae** — Hypothetical / Counterfactual Conditional

_(“under the possibility that”)_

**Meaning:** The result is considered under an **unreal, uncertain, or contrary-to-fact** condition.  
**Domain:** speculative, philosophical, reflective, narrative.  
**Use:** counterfactuals, regret, thought experiments.

**Form**

`X nae Y`

**Example (counterfactual)**

`Peace kisal nae war na-vesala`

> Peace would exist, if war had not endured.

---

## 4. Tense Interaction

|Condition Clause Tense|Interpretation|
|---|---|
|Non-past|Open / possible|
|Past|Counterfactual / unreal|

Applies to **sare**, **kel**, and **nae** (most common with **nae**).

---

## 5. Requests (Canonized)

> **Requests in Isathes are expressed as conditional constructions, never as imperatives or invocatives.**

**Preferred particles:**

- **kel** (polite, evaluative)
    
- **sare** (neutral, practical)
    

**Explicitly excluded:**

- Imperative mood
    
- Invocative mood
    
- Binding or coercive constructions
    

This guarantees requests are **non-binding**, **non-coercive**, and **ethically legible**.

---

## 6. Canon Summary

> **Isathes expresses conditional and hypothetical relations using clause-medial particles placed between a resultant clause and a condition clause. Three particles are distinguished: `sare` (factual contingency), `kel` (evaluative/social conditional), and `nae` (hypothetical or counterfactual). Conditional constructions assert dependency without asserting causation and prohibit invocative mood. Requests are expressed as conditionals to avoid command or binding force.**

# Conditional Scope: Negation, `Ve`, Cessation, and Boundary (Isathes) — **Canon**

## 1. Conditional Scope

Conditional constructions in Isathes establish a **hypothetical evaluative scope**. Within this scope, statements describe **dependency and possibility**, not enacted change.

Conditionals therefore suspend commitment to reality.

---

## 2. Negation under Conditional Scope

Negation particles (`na-`, `nal`, `uin`, `sar`) may appear freely within both the result clause and the condition clause of a conditional construction.

All negation within a conditional remains **hypothetical** and does not assert actual non-existence, loss, or cessation.

`X na-kisal sare Y`

> X would not exist, if Y holds.

Negation under conditional scope is **descriptive**, not enacted.

---

## 3. Use of `Ve` under Conditional Scope

The particle `Ve` may appear within conditional constructions **only in descriptive roles**.

### Permitted uses

- marking relational or institutional affected participants
    
- describing existing constraints, bindings, or states
    

### Prohibited uses

- resultative constructions
    
- cessative constructions
    
- any structure that would enact change or causation
    

**Rule:**

> `Ve` may not be used in resultative or cessative constructions under conditional scope.

---

## 4. Cessation under Conditional Scope

Explicit cessation constructions (e.g. `X ve na-vesal`, `X ve na-kisal`, `X ve na-thesal`) are **not permitted** within conditional constructions.

Cessation is defined in Isathes as **agentless enacted change**. Conditional scope, by contrast, evaluates hypothetical dependency and therefore cannot contain enacted change.

### Prohibited pattern

`❌ X ve na-vesal sare Y`

### Correct alternatives

`X na-vesal sare Y X vesal sare Y`

These express **hypothetical failure or continuation** without asserting enactment.

---

## 5. Boundary (`Nare`) under Conditional Scope

Boundary particles (`Nare`) may appear freely within conditional constructions to **scope the domain of hypothetical evaluation**.

Boundary under conditional scope remains descriptive and does not enact exclusion.

`X kisal sare Nare sir Y vesal`

> X exists, if beyond this boundary Y endures.

`Nare sir X kisal sare Y`

> Beyond this boundary, X would exist, if Y holds.

---

## 6. Prohibited Boundary Cessation under Conditional Scope

Constructions that enact exclusion, non-existence, or cessation across a boundary are prohibited under conditional scope.

`❌ Nare sir X ve na-kisal sare Y`

This would assert enacted exclusion while remaining hypothetical, which is grammatically invalid.

---

## 7. Summary Rule

> **Conditional constructions in Isathes evaluate dependency without enacting change. Negation and boundary scoping are permitted descriptively, while resultative and cessative constructions—marked by `Ve`—are prohibited under conditional scope.**
