# Definition

>**Ve marks the primary affected participant of a predicate.**  In ordinary transitive clauses, this is the **direct object**. In resultative/cessative clauses, this is the **patient of a caused state**.

## Frame A — Ordinary Transitive (Class I; Class II Conditionally)
### Structure
>Subject [NP] ve Verb

#### Interpretation
- `NP ve` = Direct Object (patient)
- Verb is transitive *lexically* (Class I) or by designation (Class II)
#### Examples
`vaethan ve faieth`: Create the binding (ontologically)
`Thirvingirnan ve ordar`: Establish the role / title of Thirvingirnan (institutionally)

#### Rule
>Ve here marks a true syntactic object. The verb class determines legality.

## Frame B — Resultative/Cessative (Negated Statives Only)

### Structure
`Subject [NP] ve na-[STATIVE]`
Where `[STATIVE] ∈ { vesal, kisal, thesal }`

#### Interpretation
- `NP ve` = affected subject entering the negated state
- the stative verb remains intrasitive
- the construction is **derived causation**, not transitivity
#### Examples
- `[thing] ve na-vesal`: The thing is made to not endure; the thing is ended (by a force or person)
- `[thing] ve na-kisal`: The thing is made non-present (light nonexistence)
- `[thing] ve na-thesal`: The thing is made ontologically nonexistent; complete death.
#### Rule
>Ve here marks the patient of a caused state. This does not grant object taking ability to the stative verb

## Constraint

Unless a verb class explicitly licenses Frame A, the only legal `ve` usage is Frame B with **negated statives in the allowed list.**

## Agentless Causation

Cessation frames often omit who did it. This is supported **without vagueness** by treating causation as a *grammatical operator.*

### 1. Core Rule
>The resultative/cessative `ve` + `na-[STATIVE]` frame asserts that causation occurred, but does not assert an agent.

This is **not** "nobody did it." It is:
- "an agent is not specified"
- "the cause may be impersonal"
- "the speaker is not committing to who"
### 2. Default Interpretation

When the agent is omitted, `Isathes` assumes:

> **cause exists but is out of scope.**

Possible sources include:
- environment/entropy
- institutional systems
- fate / metaphysical order
- unknown actor
- deliberate withholding (pragmatic choice)
It is controlled non-commitment.

### Cessation and Class IVb Verbs

>IVb verbs do not "stop" by tense. They must be released, broke, or rendered void as a result.

We express:
- the binding condition no longer holds
- the consequence state has changed
### Class IVb Cessation Paths

#### A. Negation of Consequence

Conceptually:
- "the bond" (Class I Result Nominal: `vaethar` / `vaethen`)
- apply cessation
>`vaethar ve na-vesal` -> the binding is made to not endure

Thus
- the bond no longer endures
- obligation dissolves
Indicating the obligation ends because *the binding ends*, not because the speaker claims it is over.
