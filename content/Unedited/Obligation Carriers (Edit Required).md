## 1. What “canonical obligation carriers” means (plain explanation)

An **obligation carrier** is **not the obligation itself**.

In Isathes:

- **IVb verbs** (_unel, vaenel_) encode _ongoing alignment / obligation_
- They **do not begin or end by tense**
- They **cannot be acted upon directly**

So when an obligation starts or ends, _something else_ must change.

That “something else” is what I called a **carrier**.

### Plain definition

> **An obligation carrier is a noun or state whose continued endurance is what makes an obligation apply.**

>Obligations in Isathes do not begin or end directly. Instead, they apply insofar as their carriers endure. Obligation carriers are constructs such as bonds, oaths, offices, or doctrines, which may constrain an individual via `se`. When a carrier ceases—whether by loss of endurance, removal of existence, or ontological termination—the obligation dissolves as a consequence.

>The construction `X se Y ve na-[STATIVE]` is grammatical and canonical, expressing that X was constrained by Y, and that Y has been caused to cease in the specified stative sense.

If the carrier endures → the obligation applies  
If the carrier ceases → the obligation dissolves

You are never “ending the obligation.”  
You are **ending what made the obligation binding**.

That distinction is crucial, and you already enforce it.

## 2. What _counts_ as an obligation carrier (canonically)

Based on everything you’ve built so far, the system already implies a closed-ish set.

### Canonical obligation carriers (current)

These are **states or constructs that can bind someone via `se`**:

1. **Bindings / bonds**
    
    - Class I result nominals
        
    - e.g. _vaethar_ (concrete bond), _vaethen_ (binding condition)
        
2. **Oaths / vows**
    
    - (to be lexically defined later)
        
    - functionally similar to bonds, but ritual / personal
        
3. **Offices / roles**
    
    - Class II institutional nominals (_ordarthir_)
        
    - obligation exists _while the office is recognized_
        
4. **Doctrines / formal positions**
    
    - Class IVa formal nominals (_-mirthir_)
        
    - obligation exists _while adherence is enforced_
        

### What is _not_ an obligation carrier

- **Will itself** (_unel_)
    
- **Obligation itself** (_vaenel_)
    
- **Belief itself** (_lamir_)
    

These are **states of alignment**, not things that can end.

## 3. Why “ending implies a type of release” (clarified)

This phrase does **not** mean:

- that different endings magically do different things
    
- that grammar decides morality
    
- that “release” is a verb hiding somewhere
    

It means something much simpler and more precise:

> **Different cessation constructions assert different reasons why an obligation no longer applies.**

In other words:  
_how_ the carrier ceased tells you _why_ the obligation ended.

### The three release logics (already implicit)

#### A. Endurance ceased → obligation expired

`[bond] ve na-vesal`

Meaning:

- the binding no longer endures
    
- therefore, anyone constrained by it is no longer constrained
    

This is:

- expiration
    
- exhaustion
    
- decay
    
- completion
    

No one necessarily “released” anyone.

---

#### B. Existence removed → obligation voided

`[bond] ve na-kisal`

Meaning:

- the binding is no longer present or available
    
- obligation cannot apply
    

This is:

- removal
    
- loss
    
- disappearance
    
- annulment by absence
    

---

#### C. Ontological termination → obligation annihilated

`[bond] ve na-thesal`

Meaning:

- the binding no longer exists in any sense
    
- the obligation cannot even be referenced
    

This is:

- death
    
- erasure
    
- metaphysical nullification
    

Rare, heavy, probably ritual.
