# Invocative Mood & Framing (Isathes) — Canon

## 1. Invocative Mood (Recap)

> **The invocative mood expresses agentless causation: the assertion that a state, relation, or result is brought into being by declaration or alignment, rather than by commanding an agent.**

Invocative clauses:

- do not address a listener
    
- do not presume compliance
    
- treat reality, law, or cosmology as the addressee
    
- assert responsibility for the utterance itself
    

Invocative mood is distinct from imperative and from tense.

---

## 2. Invocative Suffixes (Register Distinction)

Invocative mood is realized by two suffixes, which encode **register**, not grammatical force.

### 2.1 Low (Practical) Invocative — `-i`

**Form**

`Verb → Verb-i`

**Function**  
Used for ordinary but binding declarations.

**Typical contexts**

- oath creation
    
- legal or contractual speech
    
- binding declarations
    
- operative utterances
    

**Properties**

- compact and sharp
    
- socially binding
    
- witnesses implicit
    
- responsibility attaches to the speaker
    

**Definition**

> **The suffix `-i` marks the low invocative mood, asserting that a result is brought into being through declaration without ritual elevation.**

---

### 2.2 High (Ritual) Invocative — `-aen`

**Form**

`Verb → Verb-aen`

**Function**  
Used for declarations with elevated social, institutional, or cosmological weight.

**Typical contexts**

- ritual or institutional acts
    
- beyond-life oaths
    
- exile, sanctuary, or absolute boundaries
    
- curses, wards, mythic acts
    

**Properties**

- phonetically heavy and resonant
    
- socially visible
    
- implies witness, record, or cosmological notice
    
- cannot be mistaken for ordinary speech
    

**Definition**

> **The suffix `-aen` marks the high invocative mood, asserting agentless causation with elevated ontological and social weight.**

---

## 3. Verb-Class Restrictions

|Verb Class|`-i`|`-aen`|
|---|---|---|
|Class I (Causative / Creation)|✔ allowed|✔ allowed|
|Class II (Designation)|⚠ authority-dependent|⚠ ritual / institutional|
|Class III (Existence)|✖ forbidden|⚠ ritual only|
|Class IVa (Cognitive states)|✖ forbidden|✖ forbidden|
|Class IVb (Will / obligation)|✖ forbidden|✖ forbidden|

Invocative mood cannot invoke belief, will, or obligation directly.

---

## 4. Invocative Framing Particles (No Stacking)

### 4.1 Function

> **Invocative framing particles are clause-level operators that specify the grounding of an invocative declaration.**

They indicate _why_ or _on what basis_ the invocation claims legitimacy.

They:

- apply to the entire clause
    
- do not modify verbs or other particles
    
- do not replace the invocative suffix
    

---

### 4.2 Constraint (Critical)

> **An invocative clause may contain at most one invocative framing particle.**

Framing particles are **mutually exclusive** and do not stack.

If no framing particle is present, the invocation uses **default grounding**.

---

## 5. Framing Options (Canonical Set)

### 5.1 Unmarked (Default Grounding)

**Form**

`… Verb-i / Verb-aen`

**Meaning**

- personal responsibility
    
- ordinary social grounding
    
- implicit witness
    

Used for:

- private or informal oaths
    
- everyday binding speech
    

---

### 5.2 `Ven` — Authority-Grounded Invocation

**Form**

`Ven … Verb-i / Verb-aen`

**Meaning**

> The invocation is grounded in recognized authority or responsibility.

Used for:

- law
    
- offices
    
- judgments
    
- sanctioned rites
    

---

### 5.3 `Tha` — Witness-Grounded Invocation

**Form**

`Tha … Verb-i / Verb-aen`

**Meaning**

> The invocation is grounded in public or ritual witness.

Used for:

- marriages
    
- treaties
    
- communal oaths
    

Authority may exist socially but is not grammatically asserted.

---

### 5.4 `Nare [boundary]` — Boundary-Grounded Invocation

**Form**

`Nare [boundary] … Verb-i / Verb-aen`

**Meaning**

> The invocation is grounded in spatial, social, temporal, or conceptual boundary.

Used for:

- exile
    
- sanctuary
    
- wards
    
- exclusions
    

---

### 5.5 `Orin` — Cosmology-Grounded Invocation

**Form**

`Orin … Verb-aen`

**Meaning**

> The invocation is grounded in alignment with reality itself.

Used for:

- mythic acts
    
- existential change
    
- beyond-law or beyond-society declarations
    

This framing is rare and severe.

---

## 6. Relationship to Imperative and Cessation

- **Imperative (`-e`)** commands an agent.
    
- **Invocative (`-i`, `-aen`)** asserts agentless causation.
    
- **Resultative cessation** asserts agentless termination of a state.
    

Invocative and cessation form a deliberate grammatical symmetry:

- invocative causes states to begin
    
- cessation causes states to end
    

---

## 7. Canon Summary

> **Invocative mood in Isathes is marked by the suffixes `-i` (low register) and `-aen` (high register), expressing agentless causation. Invocative clauses may optionally be framed by a single clause-level grounding particle (`Ven`, `Tha`, `Nare`, or `Orin`), which specifies the basis on which the invocation claims legitimacy. Framing particles do not stack, and unmarked invocations default to personal and ordinary social grounding.**


# Aspect + Invocative Mood

### The core tension

- **Invocative mood**: _brings about_ a state (agentless causation)
    
- **Aspect**: _describes how a state unfolds over time_
    

So the question is:

> Can you describe the temporal texture of something **at the moment you are causing it to be**?

If you allow this freely, you risk:

- half-invoked states
    
- “ongoing creation”
    
- metaphysical mush
    

Isathes very clearly _does not like that_.

---

## 1.1 The clean solution: asymmetric compatibility

> **Aspect does not modify the invocative act.  
> Aspect may only modify the _resulting state_.**

That gives us a simple rule:

### ❌ Forbidden

`ren vaethi sha vaethaen sel vaethi`

These would imply:

- “I am currently binding (invocatively)”
    
- “I am beginning to invoke”
    
- “I habitually invoke”
    

All of these are ontologically incoherent in Isathes.

---

## 1.2 What _is_ allowed

You _can_ do this:

1. **Invoke**
    
2. **Then describe the resulting state with aspect**
    

Grammatically, this means:

- aspect particles **cannot appear in the same clause** as an invocative verb
    
- but they can appear in subsequent clauses describing the result
    

### Example

`Bond vaethi. Bond ren vesal.`

> Let the bond be established.  
> The bond is now holding.

This preserves:

- invocative sharpness
    
- aspect clarity
    
- temporal honesty
    

---

## 1.3 Canon rule (invocative + aspect)

> **Aspect particles do not modify invocative verbs. Aspect may only describe the endurance or behavior of states resulting from an invocative act and must appear in a separate clause.**

This mirrors your rule about **no framing conflicts** and keeps invocations clean and atomic.

---

# 2) Aspect + Obligation / Binding

This one is subtler — and very powerful.

Recall:

- **vaeth** (Class I): to bind
    
- **vaenel** (Class IVb): to be obligated (by binding, oath, duty)
    
- bindings endure (`vesal`) unless released
    
- obligations are carried, not enacted
    

Aspect fits _beautifully_ here.

---

## 2.1 Aspect applies to _endurance of bindings_, not their creation

You do **not** aspectualize:

- the act of binding (`vaeth`)
    
- the act of release (future verb)
    

You aspectualize:

- whether the binding **holds**
    
- whether the obligation **is characteristic, ongoing, or emerging**
    

---

## 2.2 Core patterns

### A) Ongoing obligation

`Oath ve vaenel ren kisal`

> The oath-obligation is currently in force.

### B) Habitual obligation (custom / role-based)

`Office ve vaenel sel kisal`

> The office carries customary obligations.

This is _excellent_ for social structure.

---

### C) Emerging obligation

`Oath ve vaenel sha kisal`

> The obligation is coming into force.

This gives you:

- probationary periods
    
- transitional authority
    
- rites-in-progress
    

All without invoking or predicting.

---

## 2.3 Aspect + instability (binding under stress)

This is where the system shines.

### Obligation under threat

`Oath ve vaenel ren kisal. Oath ve vaenel brethsalir kisal.`

> The obligation is currently in force.  
> Failure is present with respect to the obligation.

### Fragile obligation

`Oath ve vaenel karethir nal kisal`

> The obligation lacks support.

Aspect lets you say **how it is holding**, instability lets you say **why it might fail**.

---

## 2.4 Canon rule (obligation + aspect)

> **Aspect particles may modify the endurance of bindings and obligations, describing whether they are ongoing (`ren`), customary (`sel`), or emerging (`sha`). Aspect does not modify the act of binding itself, nor does it enact or release obligation.**

---

# Final canon-ready summary (both points)

You can paste this as a combined rule:

> **Aspect particles in Isathes do not modify invocative verbs or acts of causation. Aspect applies only to the endurance or behavior of resulting states and must appear in a separate clause. Aspect interacts productively with bindings and obligations, allowing speakers to describe whether obligations are ongoing, customary, or emerging without asserting creation or release.**

Aspect applies to the endurance or existence of bindings expressed via nominalized Class I forms (e.g. `vaethen`), not to IVb obligation verbs such as `vaenel`. Obligations are carried by bindings; therefore, aspectual particles (`ren`, `sel`, `sha`) modify the binding as an enduring state (`kisal` / `vesal`), rather than the volitional relation itself.
