In Isathes, **aspect is not about events** in the Indo-European sense.  
It is about **how a state of endurance behaves over time**.

Aspect answers questions like:

- Is this endurance **currently holding**?
    
- Is it **customary / characteristic**?
    
- Is it **coming into being**?
    

Aspect does **not**:

- assert causation
    
- predict the future
    
- enact change
    
- override tense or mood
    

Aspect is **speaker framing of endurance**.

---

# 2. The Three Endurance-Aspect Particles

All three are:

- particles (not suffixes)
    
- clause-level
    
- non-stacking
    
- compatible with non-past and past
    
- compatible with conditionals
    
- incompatible with invocative framing conflicts
    

---

## A. **ren** — Progressive / Ongoing Endurance

_(“currently holding”)_

### Meaning

> The state is presently ongoing and has not lapsed.

This is the most important aspect for Isathes.

### Use

- current endurance
    
- holding states
    
- ongoing processes
    

### Form

`ren vesal`

### Examples

`Bond ren vesal`

> The bond is currently holding.

`Peace ren kisal`

> Peace is presently in effect.

`Ward ren vesala`

> The ward was holding (ongoing in the past).

---

## B. **sel** — Habitual / Characteristic Endurance

_(“tends to endure”)_

### Meaning

> The state customarily or characteristically holds.

This is **not** the same as “always”.  
It encodes **pattern**, not certainty.

### Use

- laws
    
- customs
    
- traits
    
- long-term expectations
    

### Form

`sel vesal`

### Examples

`Law sel vesal`

> The law generally endures.

`Trust sel kisal`

> Trust tends to exist.

**Note:**  
`sel` as aspect is grammatical; `sel` as “hello” is pragmatic/discourse. Context disambiguates naturally.

---

## C. **sha** — Inceptive / Onset of Endurance

_(“beginning to hold”)_

### Meaning

> The state is entering endurance.

This is **non-causative**.  
It does _not_ assert that something was made to happen.

### Use

- beginnings
    
- emergent states
    
- early stability
    

### Form

`sha vesal`

### Examples

`Bond sha vesal`

> The bond is beginning to hold.

`Peace sha kisal`

> Peace is coming into being.

`Order sha vesala`

> Order had begun to take hold.

# 3. Rules for Endurance-Aspect (Canon)

### Rule 1 — Non-stacking

Only **one aspect particle** may appear per clause.

### Rule 2 — Aspect ≠ causation

Aspect particles:

- do not invoke
    
- do not command
    
- do not cessate
    

### Rule 3 — Compatibility

Aspect particles may appear with:

- non-past
    
- past (`-a`)
    
- conditional constructions
    

Aspect particles **must not** override invocative framing.  
(If you invoke endurance, you do not also “frame” how it unfolds.)

---

# 4. Aspect + Instability (important interaction)

Aspect lets you talk about **how instability behaves**, not whether it resolves.

### Examples

`brethsalir ren kisal`

> Failure is currently ongoing.

`karethir nal ren kisal`

> Lack of support is currently present.

`City ve enemy ren dresal`

> The enemy is actively opposing the city.

`City ren dramir`

> The city is currently in a threatened state.

This is _exactly_ the expressive gap we identified earlier.

---

# 5. Aspect + Conditionals (sanity check)

Aspect is allowed inside conditionals because it remains descriptive.

`Bond na-vesal sare brethsalir ren kisal`

> The bond would not endure if failure were ongoing.

No enactment. No violation.

### Endurance Aspect (Isathes) — Canon

> **Isathes marks aspect using clause-level particles that frame how endurance behaves over time. The particle `ren` marks ongoing or currently holding endurance, `sel` marks habitual or characteristic endurance, and `sha` marks the onset or beginning of endurance. Aspect particles do not assert causation, do not enact change, and do not stack. They are compatible with tense, conditionals, and instability constructions, and serve to describe the temporal texture of enduring states.**
