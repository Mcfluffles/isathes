# Instability and Precarious States

Isathes distinguishes **non-causative** instability states from **enacted cessation**. Instability constructions describe _current conditions of endurance_ without asserting change, prediction, or causation, and remain compatible with conditional scope.

#### A. Active Failure (failure pressure present)

**Construction**

`[failure-nominal] kisal`

**Example**

`brethsalir kisal`

**Meaning:** Failure exists / is present (failure is underway), without asserting cessation.

---

#### B. Structural Fragility (support absent)

Structural fragility is expressed as the **absence** of stabilizing conditions.

**Construction**

`[stabilizing-nominal] nal kisal`

**Example**

`karethir nal kisal`

**Meaning:** Support is absent (endurance may still exist, but is poorly supported).

---

#### C. External Threat (objective opposition)

External threat is modeled as **objective opposition** via Class III.

**Verb**

- `dresal` (Class III): to oppose / to threaten another (external condition)

**Descriptive affected participant (`Ve` looks left)**

`X ve Y dresal`

**Example**

`[City] ve [enemy] dresal`

**Meaning:** The city is threatened by an enemy (opposition exists).

---

#### D. Threatened State (internal vulnerability / perception)

Threatenedness is modeled as an **internal state** via Class IVa, using the same root.

**Verb**

- `dramir` (Class IVa): to feel threatened / to be in a threatened state

**Examples**

`[City] dramir`
`ei dramir`

**Meaning:** Vulnerability is felt/recognized (does not assert objective opposition).

---

#### E. Enacted Cessation (contrast)

Cessation is an **enacted change** and is distinct from all instability expressions.

**Example**

`Bond ve na-vesal`

**Meaning:** The bond has been caused to cease.

---

#### F. Key compatibility note

- Instability expressions are **descriptive**, not enacted.
- They may appear under conditionals (`sare / kel / nae`) without violating the “no cessation in hypotheticals” rule.
