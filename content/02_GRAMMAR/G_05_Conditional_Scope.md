# Conditional Scope: Negation, `Ve`, Cessation, and Boundary

## 1. Conditional Scope

Conditional constructions in Isathes establish a **hypothetical evaluative scope**. Within this scope, statements describe **dependency and possibility**, not enacted change.

Conditionals therefore suspend commitment to reality.

---

## 2. Negation under Conditional Scope

Negation particles (`na-`, `nal`, `uin`, `sar`) may appear freely within both the result clause and the condition clause of a conditional construction.

All negation within a conditional remains **hypothetical** and does not assert actual non-existence, loss, or cessation.

`X na-kisal sare Y`

> X would not exist, if Y holds.

Negation under conditional scope is **descriptive**, not enacted.

---

## 3. Use of `Ve` under Conditional Scope

The particle `Ve` may appear within conditional constructions **only in descriptive roles**.

### Permitted uses

- marking relational or institutional affected participants
- describing existing constraints, bindings, or states

### Prohibited uses

- resultative constructions
- cessative constructions
- any structure that would enact change or causation

**Rule:**

> `Ve` may not be used in resultative or cessative constructions under conditional scope.

---

## 4. Cessation under Conditional Scope

Explicit cessation constructions (e.g. `X ve na-vesal`, `X ve na-kisal`, `X ve na-thesal`) are **not permitted** within conditional constructions.

Cessation is defined in Isathes as **agentless enacted change**. Conditional scope, by contrast, evaluates hypothetical dependency and therefore cannot contain enacted change.

### Prohibited pattern

`X ve na-vesal sare Y`

### Correct alternatives

`X na-vesal sare Y`
`X vesal sare Y`

These express **hypothetical failure or continuation** without asserting enactment.

---

## 5. Boundary (`Nare`) under Conditional Scope

Boundary particles (`Nare`) may appear freely within conditional constructions to **scope the domain of hypothetical evaluation**.

Boundary under conditional scope remains descriptive and does not enact exclusion.

`X kisal sare Nare sir Y vesal`

> X exists, if beyond this boundary Y endures.

`Nare sir X kisal sare Y`

> Beyond this boundary, X would exist, if Y holds.

---

## 6. Prohibited Boundary Cessation under Conditional Scope

Constructions that enact exclusion, non-existence, or cessation across a boundary are prohibited under conditional scope.

`Nare sir X ve na-kisal sare Y`

This would assert enacted exclusion while remaining hypothetical, which is grammatically invalid.

---

## 7. Summary

> **Conditional constructions in Isathes evaluate dependency without enacting change. Negation and boundary scoping are permitted descriptively, while resultative and cessative constructions—marked by `Ve`—are prohibited under conditional scope.**
